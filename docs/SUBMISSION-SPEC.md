# Strigoi — Designer Photography Submission Spec

**STATUS: DRAFT — pending founder approval. Not yet sent to any designer. Nothing
in this document is binding until the founder signs off.**

This document tells you, an invited Strigoi designer, exactly what to send us for
your roster entry and your specimens, and what happens if what you send doesn't
match it. It exists because Strigoi's homepage holds every section to a strict
"one screen, one frame" rule — the specimens and roster sections are not
infinitely scrollable galleries, they're a fixed frame, and a photograph that
doesn't match this spec will visually break that frame for every visitor, not
just look slightly off.

If any of this is impractical for how you shoot or what gear you have, tell us —
we'd rather talk about it before you send files than reject them after.

---

## 1. What we need from you

### Roster portrait (one image)

- **Aspect ratio:** square, 1:1.
- **Dimensions:** minimum 400×400px. Maximum 2000×2000px (we'll downscale
  anything larger; there's no benefit to sending more).
- **Format:** JPG or WEBP. PNG accepted if you need transparency (see
  Background, below); avoid GIF.
- **Content:** your face, your studio's mark/logo, or a representative close
  detail of your work. This displays small (roughly the size of a thumbnail
  nail) next to your name — anything with fine detail or small text won't read
  at that size.
- **If you don't have one yet:** send nothing. We show a plain initial-letter
  mark instead until you do. There's no penalty for skipping this — it's the
  one image in this spec that's genuinely optional.
- **Light or dark:** any mark is welcome, on a light ground or a dark one. The
  card it sits on adapts to it; you don't need to make a dark version.

### Specimen photographs (one per piece, up to 3 initially)

- **Aspect ratio: 4:3 or wider (landscape), OR square 1:1.**
  **Do not send portrait-orientation photographs (taller than wide) for
  specimens.** We tested this: a portrait 3:4 photograph at our column width is
  taller than the screen space we have for it, and a page of three overflows it
  by 3x. Landscape and square are the only orientations that currently fit our
  layout at normal screen sizes. If your piece is inherently a portrait subject
  (e.g. a long garment shot head-to-hem), see the note at the end of this
  section — the Bestiary shows it in its own shape (§2, item 4); for the
  homepage we'll ask for a square or landscape alternate (see §3).
- **On the homepage your photograph is shown in a wide window, 16:9**, filled
  edge to edge. A wider photograph fits it best. A square one shows only about
  55–60% of itself there, a band across its middle, so the top and the foot of
  the piece can be cut. Tell us where the piece sits in the picture and we'll
  place the window there, or send a wider shot.
- **Dimensions:** minimum 800px on the short edge. Maximum 3000px on the long
  edge.
- **Format:** JPG or WEBP, sRGB color profile.
- **Background:** see §2 below — this is the part most likely to need a
  conversation.
- **Count:** up to 3 specimens per designer at launch. This number is a current
  layout constraint, not a judgment of how much work you have — we're actively
  reviewing whether it can grow (see §4, "what happens as this evolves").

**How your photograph sits in the shop.** On your shop's wall every photograph is
shown in the same upright frame, 4 wide by 5 tall, filled edge to edge: a square
photograph loses a little of each side, a landscape one more. Keep the piece
whole inside the middle of your picture, or tell us where its centre is and
we'll hold the crop there. On the page about the piece alone, your photograph is
shown whole. Close-ups and full views are both welcome: a close-up fills its
frame and reads larger and brighter on the wall, a full view with dark ground
round it reads quieter. Light grounds are welcome in the shop, and everywhere
else (§2).

## 2. Background and color

**You don't have to shoot on a dark ground.** We can't, and won't, ask makers to
send only dark photographs. Everywhere on Strigoi (your shop, the page about your
piece alone, the Bestiary and the homepage's specimens) a photograph on a white,
bright or high-key ground is welcome, and it is shown in its own colours. Where
our words sit over a light photograph (a name, a caption, a price), keeping them
readable is our job, not yours: we hold them at a contrast of at least 4.5:1 by
our own means, such as a darker band behind the words, and never by changing
your photograph.

**What we ask, place by place:**

1. **Any ground, dark or light, in every place.** A dark studio backdrop, a
   shadowed surface, natural dim light, a white sweep or a bright window: send
   the photograph as you shot it; nothing further needed.
2. **White, bright, or high-key studio backgrounds are welcome in your shop, on
   your piece's page, in the Bestiary and on the homepage.** You don't need to
   re-shoot against a darker backdrop for any of them. On the homepage your
   piece stands in its own dark box, so a light photograph reads as a lit print
   in a dark frame, not as a hole in the page.
3. **We will not apply a color-grade, duotone, or tint to your photography.**
   We tested that option and rejected it — it makes your work look like it
   was filtered into a house style, which isn't the deal we're offering
   designers. On the homepage we add no vignette either (no darkening at its
   edges): the dark box round your photograph is its frame.
4. **How the homepage and the Bestiary show your photograph.** Both show it
   untorn, with its plain edges, in its own colours, each piece in its own
   niche, like a piece in a cabinet of curiosities. We don't tint, grade or
   filter it.
   On the homepage, one specimen is shown at a time, in one still box on the red
   wall, lit like a magic lantern's slide: the photographs slide through the box
   one after another, slowly, and a visitor can pause them. Your photograph fills
   the box's wide 16:9 window (see §1 for what that means for a square
   photograph). Pointing at it holds it still; it never changes how your
   photograph looks.
   In the Bestiary every piece has its own niche in the cabinet's shelves. Your
   photograph keeps its own shape (tall, wide or square); if it isn't one of our
   nine frame shapes, we trim it slightly to the nearest one. Its size on the wall
   changes every day, as does its place.
   Everywhere else, including the shop and any page about your piece alone, your
   photograph is shown as you sent it (on the shop's wall, in the upright frame
   described in §1).

## 3. What happens if you send something non-conforming

- **Wrong aspect ratio (portrait):** for the homepage we'll ask if you have a
  landscape or square alternate. If not, your photograph is shown in the
  Bestiary only — we will not silently crop or stretch your photograph
  without asking.
- **Bright/white/high-key background:** this is not non-conforming anywhere:
  it is welcome in your shop, on your piece's page, in the Bestiary and on the
  homepage, and you don't need to re-shoot or re-send (§2). We do not apply a
  color filter to it (see §2).
- **Below minimum resolution:** we'll ask for a re-send. We won't upscale a
  low-resolution file; it will look worse than the surrounding page, not
  better.
- **File too large / wrong format:** we'll convert it on our end; no need to
  resend for this alone.
- **Missing entirely:** the roster photo is optional (see §1). A specimen
  photograph is not — a specimen without an image doesn't currently have a
  defined fallback in our design, so we'll follow up with you directly rather
  than publish a gap.

## 4. What happens as this evolves — please read this part

This spec reflects a layout constraint we found by testing real photographs
against our actual page, not an arbitrary preference. The short version: our
homepage promises "one section fits one screen," and photographs are much
taller (relative to their width) than the plain text rows we designed against
originally. The numbers, if you're curious: a single portrait photograph at our
column width already needs more vertical space than a laptop screen gives us;
three specimens (our current per-designer count) needs three times that.

We are actively deciding, as a platform, whether to:

- keep this spec's constraints permanently (landscape/square only, capped
  count), or
- change the page layout itself to accommodate a wider range of photography.

Either way, **we will not change what's expected of you without telling you
first and giving you the chance to re-supply.** If the layout changes and your
existing photography works fine, you'll hear nothing. If it doesn't, we'll
reach out before anything changes on the live page.
